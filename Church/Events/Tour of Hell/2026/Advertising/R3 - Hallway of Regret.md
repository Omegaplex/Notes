# R3 — Hallway of Regret

Read and follow the shared Tour of Hell Facebook advertising standard in:

Church/Events/Tour of Hell/2026/Advertising/Facebook hype ads.md

Apply that file’s required format, composition, typography hierarchy, hand-drawn concept-art treatment, restrained theatrical scale, biblical tone, and updated TOUROFHELL.CC footer treatment.

Then use the following room-specific direction for Room 3 — Hallway of Regret.

SCENE BASIS

Base the image on the current Room 3 script and its biblical references:

- Hebrews 2:3
- 2 Corinthians 6:2

The central ideas are:

- neglected salvation
- regret after missed opportunity
- the realization that time was wasted
- the warning that now is the accepted time
- the sorrow of those who heard truth but did not respond

This image should represent a symbolic, theatrical visualization of regret. It should not imply that there is a literal biblical “hallway of regret.” The scene is an artistic warning built around the emotional and spiritual message of the room.

UPPER THIRD TEXT

Use:

I THOUGHT
I HAD MORE TIME.

CENTRAL VISUAL

Show a narrow, dark hallway inspired by the real Hallway of Regret set.

Do not reveal the room’s wall-of-faces surprise.

The wall should appear mostly solid, dark, and unreadable. It may have faint texture or subtle irregularity, but it should not clearly reveal masks, faces, mounted objects, or obvious details.

The orange glow should not come from clearly visible eyes or obvious wall features. Instead, let the warm orange light come from off-screen, from deeper in the hallway, or from low hidden sources outside the viewer’s clear line of sight.

The light should cast an uneasy glow across the hallway and create the feeling that something is present beyond what can be fully seen.

Let the hallway appear to continue much farther than the real space, fading gradually into darkness.

Along the lower part of the hallway, partially hidden in darkness, show a few vague sorrowful human figures or silhouettes seated low against the wall or on the floor.

These figures may have subtle chains or restraints, but they should remain secondary, shadowed, and understated.

Keep the opposite side of the hallway simple and mostly dark.

Do not include any table, covered table, pedestal, stand, furniture grouping, draped object, or unidentified prop on the right side of the hallway.

The right side should remain visually clean and dark so the viewer’s attention stays on the hallway depth, orange glow, and sorrowful figures.

VISUAL EMPHASIS

The image should communicate:

- regret
- missed opportunity
- too-late realization
- sorrow
- the overwhelming sense that these souls could have responded, but did not

Use darkness, depth, low orange illumination, shadowed figures, and a sense of unseen presence as the main storytelling devices.

The mood should be sorrowful, haunting, and spiritually serious — not grotesque, not gory, and not like a horror-movie dungeon.

ENVIRONMENT

Use room-specific elements such as:

- a dark enclosed hallway
- black draped ceiling or shadowed overhead space
- one wall that appears solid, dark, and mysterious
- warm orange glow from off-screen or hidden low sources
- heavy darkness around the edges
- a few subtle pools of low orange light
- faint haze or atmospheric depth if helpful
- a small number of shadowed human figures low to the ground
- an uncluttered dark wall on the opposite side

The hallway should feel tight and enclosed near the viewer, then visually stretch farther into darkness.

BIBLICAL EMPHASIS

The image should visually support the message of:

“How shall we escape, if we neglect so great a salvation?”
— Hebrews 2:3

and

“Behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.”
— 2 Corinthians 6:2

The scene should feel like a warning about neglected salvation and future regret, not a literal doctrinal depiction of a specific chamber in the afterlife.

LOWER THIRD WEBSITE TREATMENT

Use:

TOUROFHELL.CC

Render the website in the shared campaign footer style:

- large elegant distressed serif lettering
- warm ivory / cream text
- subtle orange glow or illumination
- centered on a dark lower background
- framed by thin horizontal fire-like glowing lines above and below
- the framing lines should look like restrained ember/fire accents, not large flames
- dramatic and refined, while remaining very readable on a phone

DO NOT INCLUDE

- visible masks
- readable faces on the wall
- visible mirror panels
- obvious reflected room geometry
- glowing eyes clearly embedded in faces
- tables
- covered tables
- pedestals
- stands
- draped furniture
- unidentified props on the right side
- fantasy dungeon architecture
- torture devices
- gore
- monsters
- demons
- elaborate prison cells
- exaggerated chains everywhere
- medieval torture chamber imagery
- giant fantasy landscapes
- oversized screaming faces
- literal flames filling the hallway
- anything that turns the room into generic haunted-house imagery

Keep the image grounded in the real room’s emotional identity while preserving the surprise: a dark narrow passage, low sorrowful figures, off-screen orange glow, a clean uncluttered opposite wall, and the sense that regret stretches endlessly into darkness.

The finished image should communicate the terrible sorrow of realizing too late that salvation was neglected, while remaining clearly symbolic, theatrical, visually tied to the actual Hallway of Regret set, and careful not to spoil the room’s most shocking reveal.
