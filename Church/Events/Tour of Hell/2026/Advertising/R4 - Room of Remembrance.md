# R4 — Room of Remembrance

Read and follow the shared Tour of Hell Facebook advertising standard in:

Church/Events/Tour of Hell/2026/Advertising/Facebook hype ads.md

Apply that file’s required format, composition, typography hierarchy, hand-drawn concept-art treatment, restrained theatrical scale, biblical tone, and updated TOUROFHELL.CC footer treatment.

Then use the following room-specific direction for Room 4 — Room of Remembrance.

SCENE BASIS

Base the image on the current Room 4 script and Luke 16:23–28, especially the account of the rich man in torment remembering the opportunity he had during his lifetime.

The central ideas are:

- a person had an opportunity to respond to God
- that opportunity was ignored
- the consequences of that choice are now final
- they remember the chance they once had
- they desperately wish they could go back
- a great gulf now separates them from what they once had access to

The image should communicate torment joined with remembrance and regret.

This is a symbolic theatrical interpretation of the biblical warning, not a literal claim about the exact appearance of Hell.

UPPER THIRD TEXT

Keep the upper area dark and uncluttered.

Use large, bold, condensed white text:

HE LIFTED UP HIS EYES
IN TORMENT

Directly beneath the headline, add the much smaller scripture reference:

LUKE 16:23

The scripture reference should be restrained and secondary.

CENTRAL VISUAL

Use a side-perspective composition.

The viewer should feel as though they are near the torment side, looking across a clear uncrossable gulf toward the remembered church setting.

The remembered church should be visible across the divide, with the torment side occupying the dark foreground.

THE REMEMBERED CHURCH SIDE

Represent the last opportunity the person had to respond to God as a simple old country church setting.

This should feel like a humble rural Protestant / Pentecostal church, plain and familiar rather than formal or ornate.

Include:

- old-style wooden pews
- a plain wooden pulpit
- a simple cross on the wall
- restrained red illumination
- a modest ordinary church atmosphere

IMPORTANT ROOM RELATIONSHIP:

- the pews must be straight, orderly, and properly aligned
- the pews must run parallel to one another
- the pews must clearly face the front preaching area
- the pulpit must sit at the front of the church side
- the pulpit must be turned correctly for preaching to the pews
- the pulpit should be plain, simple, wooden, and country-church in style
- the church side should feel like a simple old country church, not a formal sanctuary

IMPORTANT EXCLUSIONS FOR THE CHURCH SIDE:

- do not include any altar table
- do not include any communion table
- do not include any table at all
- do not include a table draped with a white cloth
- do not include any cloth-covered furniture
- do not show a Bible in view
- do not show an open Bible
- do not place a Bible on the pulpit
- do not include visible books of any kind as focal details
- keep the church side visually simple and uncluttered

The church side should read as:

- straight wooden pews in front
- a plain wooden pulpit at the front
- a simple cross on the wall behind
- no altar table and no visible Bible

Do not make the setup resemble a Catholic sanctuary.

Do not use:

- ornate altar styling
- cathedral architecture
- candles on an altar
- priestly or liturgical imagery
- elaborate religious furniture
- formal decorative church staging

THE LOST PERSON IN TORMENT

Place the lost person mostly inside the dark torment half.

The lost person should be reaching across the chasm from the shadows.

Show only enough of the person to establish emotion:

- mostly hidden in darkness
- only a silhouette, partial outline, or faint form visible
- perhaps one arm and hand stretching out across the divide
- maybe a bowed head or shoulder barely visible
- minimal red edge-light at most

IMPORTANT:

Do not illuminate the lost person as a major visible subject.

Do not make the figure the focal point through lighting.

The emotional effect should be that the person is swallowed by darkness, reaching out from torment toward what can no longer be reached.

THE CHASM / GREAT GULF

Make the chasm visually obvious and broad.

The divide must read clearly and immediately as a real separation.

Represent it as:

- a distinct open gulf
- a clearly readable drop
- deep blackness with visible edge definition
- strong contrast between the near edge and far edge
- unmistakably uncrossable

The chasm should be more apparent through shape, edges, and composition — not through brightly lighting the lost person.

There should be no bright illumination inside the chasm itself.

Do not fill it with glowing lava or obvious internal light.

A very subtle red infernal glow may exist low on the torment side only, but the gulf itself should remain predominantly black, empty, and lightless.

Do not use:

- glowing lava rivers
- fantasy canyon walls covered in detail
- fiery cracks throughout the divide
- visible bridges
- visible bottom
- scenic rock formations that make it look adventurous or fantasy-like

The divide should feel:

- fixed
- absolute
- empty
- final
- impossible to cross

TORMENT SIDE ATMOSPHERE

The torment side should be almost fully overtaken by darkness.

Use:

- deep blackness
- shadow
- faint smoke or haze
- minimal restrained infernal red at the margins
- oppressive emptiness

Do not overfill it with flames.

The darkness itself should communicate torment, abandonment, and separation.

ROOM CHARACTER / ARCHITECTURE

The room should feel like a simple church-basement or small country-church room adapted into a dramatic symbolic scene.

Do not copy a modern suspended grid ceiling or visible drop-ceiling tiles.

Instead, keep the ceiling treatment dark, simplified, shadowed, or visually subdued so it does not call attention to modern ceiling details.

The focus should remain on the church side, the divide, and the shadowed lost person.

VISUAL STORY

At a glance, the image should suggest:

A person hidden in the darkness of torment is reaching across an obvious, uncrossable chasm toward the simple church setting where they once had an opportunity to respond to God — but the chance is gone forever.

The image should be more about memory and irreversible loss than about spectacle.

VISUAL HIERARCHY

The strongest story elements should be:

1. the headline
2. the remembered church side with straight pews, plain pulpit, and cross
3. the clearly apparent dark chasm / great gulf
4. the lost person reaching from the shadows

The lost person should not overpower the composition.

The church side should read clearly as a remembered chance.

The chasm should read clearly as total separation.

BIBLICAL TONE

The image should support Luke 16:23–26:

the rich man is in torment, remembers his former opportunity, and discovers that a great gulf is fixed.

The emphasis should be on:

- memory
- lost opportunity
- personal responsibility
- final consequences
- irreversible separation

The tragedy is not only torment itself, but the memory of the chance that was once available.

LOWER THIRD WEBSITE TREATMENT

Use:

TOUROFHELL.CC

Render the website in the shared campaign footer style:

- large elegant distressed serif lettering
- warm ivory / cream text
- subtle orange glow or illumination
- centered on a dark lower background
- framed by thin horizontal fire-like glowing lines above and below
- restrained ember/fire accents rather than large flames
- dramatic and refined
- highly readable on a phone

DO NOT INCLUDE

- any altar table
- any communion table
- any table with a white cloth
- any table at all
- visible Bible or open Bible
- visible books
- pews that are crooked, uneven, or angled inconsistently
- pulpit facing the viewer
- ornate altar styling
- Catholic-looking sanctuary elements
- a brightly lit lost person
- the torment figure as the main focal point
- a weak or unclear divide
- a flat floor where no chasm is evident
- visible lighting inside the blacked-out divide
- glowing lava filling the separation
- fantasy castle imagery
- demonic architecture
- excessive flames
- gore
- torture devices
- a visible suspended grid ceiling
- obvious drop-ceiling tiles
- anything that turns the image into generic fantasy hell art

The finished image should feel like a hand-drawn cinematic concept-art warning based on Luke 16:23–26: from a side perspective, a mostly hidden lost person reaches out from deep shadow across a clearly apparent black chasm toward a simple old country church setting with straight pews, a plain correctly oriented pulpit, and a cross, making clear that the opportunity is gone forever.
