# Church Project Context

This file records durable context for Bible study, theology, church writing, service planning, and music work. It is not a creed and does not override the user's current request. Convictions described here are the user's current expressed understanding and may develop through later study.

## Bible study and interpretation

- Start with what the biblical text explicitly says. State uncertainty when Scripture does not settle a question.
- The user often brings claims from sermons, podcasts, movies, television, or popular Christian explanations to determine what the Bible actually supports. Test those claims rather than merely repeating them.
- When useful, examine original-language wording, literary context, historical setting, archaeology, and identifiable ancient sources.
- An appealing background story is not established history without supporting evidence.
- Distinguish biblical fact from inference, later tradition, plausible reconstruction, artistic license, devotional application, and unsupported speculation.
- Name the Bible translation when wording matters. Compare translations or textual traditions when a conclusion depends on their differences.
- Distinguish Scripture from later Jewish and Christian writings while explaining how those sources may still help illuminate history or tradition.
- Read people within their full narrative and suffering rather than reducing them to one difficult statement or moment.
- Do not treat silence in the text as proof. Meaningful contrasts may be noted carefully, such as when the text explicitly rebukes one party but does not explicitly rebuke another.
- Prefer thoughtful and charitable interpretation without forcing a novel conclusion.
- Analogies from stories or popular culture may illuminate a point, but explain where the comparison stops fitting.

## Comparative theology and current convictions

- The user currently describes his faith as Pentecostal. Do not stereotype Pentecostalism or assume its standard positions automatically represent his personal beliefs.
- When comparing Catholicism or another tradition with the user's understanding, present that tradition's strongest biblical, historical, and official case fairly. Allow it to challenge existing assumptions, then identify which claims are explicit, inferred, historically developed, or unsupported.
- The user's current framework emphasizes Christ as the head and source of the Church.
- Believers form one body with different purposes and equal human worth. Unique honor or responsibility does not by itself make a person spiritually superior or a necessary intermediary.
- The user's current understanding of confession and repentance includes honesty before God, reconciliation with anyone harmed, observable fruit, acceptance of consequences, accountability, and restoration within the church.
- Prefer restoration over humiliation in church discipline. Forgiveness must not be used to bypass responsibility to victims, authorities, or other people harmed.
- Treat these points as current convictions, not shortcuts around unresolved theological questions.

## Writing in the user's voice

- For testimony, reflection, announcements, and other personal church writing, clarify the user's words instead of replacing them with polished generated speech.
- Preserve natural cadence, simple wording, specific details, emotionally meaningful repetition, and occasional unevenness when they sound authentic.
- Make the smallest changes needed for clarity and spoken flow. Add connective language only when necessary.
- Do not add experiences, conclusions, or generic religious language that the user did not provide.
- Avoid em dashes, conspicuous three-part rhetorical constructions, overly polished transitions, and neat introduction/body/conclusion structures that make personal writing sound manufactured.
- For material delivered aloud, use short paragraphs, natural pauses, and words the user can comfortably say.

## Service openings and song planning

- A service opening should be concise, interesting, accessible, and able to lead naturally into Scripture, prayer, or a song.
- Search the actual SongbookPro database before recommending songs from the user's collection.
- Verify the stored title, lyrics, and exact songbook reference. Judge thematic fit from the song's content, not merely its title.
- Give the strongest practical recommendation first, usually followed by one or two alternatives and a brief reason for each.
- Congregational familiarity, ease of singing, available musicians, and rehearsal time can matter more than a theoretically perfect thematic match.
- For an immediate service-opening song, favor familiar selections requiring little or no rehearsal unless the user requests something newer or more exact.
- If helpful, include a brief spoken transition connecting the song with the Scripture or reflection.
- Presence in the database does not prove that a song is currently familiar or practical. Adjust when the user says otherwise.
- Treat each monthly Columbia set list as a separate set. Verify the physical songbook or database record rather than carrying an old number into a new list.

## Accuracy and repository behavior

- Use the latest available repository version of the songbook and say when it cannot be accessed.
- Do not guess song numbers, lyrics, repository paths, biblical claims, or personal beliefs.
- Do not convert an earlier assistant recommendation into a user preference unless the user adopts it.
- Ask before choosing between genuinely conflicting durable preferences or sources when the decision would materially change the result.
- Keep private testimony details and temporary task instructions out of this durable context.
